For Community Leaders & Municipal Officials

How we source, rehabilitate, and manage homes.

This page explains our acquisition criteria, rehabilitation standards, and how we handle properties that are occupied at the time of purchase. Designed for the officials and community partners who want to understand us before taking a meeting.

Property Sourcing

How we find properties.

We focus on single-family homes and duplexes in Johnston, Franklin, Wake, Lenoir, and Durham counties that meet three criteria: structurally sound but deteriorated, located in an established neighborhood rather than a greenfield site, and priced at a level where rehabilitation leads to rents that existing residents can afford.

We do not flip properties. We do not pursue portfolio acquisitions that would displace a large number of tenants at once. We work one property at a time, in neighborhoods where we are prepared to be a long-term landlord — not a short-term holder.

Sources include: municipal vacancy lists, word of mouth from community members, direct owner outreach on properties that have been listed or vacant for extended periods, and relationships with local housing attorneys and code enforcement officials who can refer properties at risk of condemnation.

Rehabilitation Standards

What "rehabbed" means to us.

Every property we rehabilitate is brought to a condition we would be comfortable renting to a family member. That is the standard. Below are the specific areas we assess and address in every project.

  • Structural integrity

    Foundation, roof, and framing inspected and repaired before any cosmetic work begins. No property goes to market with deferred structural issues.

  • Electrical and plumbing

    All work permitted and inspected by county. We do not use unlicensed contractors for electrical, plumbing, or HVAC — period. A certificate of occupancy is required before any tenant moves in.

  • HVAC and weatherization

    Functioning heating and cooling is not optional. Where budget allows, we upgrade insulation and weatherstripping — both for tenant comfort and to reduce utility costs, which affects what rents are actually reasonable.

  • Health and safety

    Lead paint testing on all pre-1978 properties. Mold remediation where found. Working smoke and CO detectors installed in every unit before occupancy.

  • Local contractor requirement

    We hire contractors who live and work in the county where the project is located. This is a hard rule, not a preference. We track it on every project.

From Acquisition to Occupancy

Our process, step by step.

01

Acquisition

We identify the property, conduct a scope-of-work assessment, and close. No assignment contracts, no wholesaling — we buy and hold.

02

Permit & Plan

We pull permits before work begins. All work is scoped in writing with licensed local contractors. Budget and timeline are set before the first nail.

03

Rehab

Work is completed to county code, inspected, and documented. We photograph every phase of the project for our records and for partner reporting.

04

Lease & Manage

We market to existing neighborhood residents first, conduct standard screening, and manage the property directly — no third-party property manager.

A Note for Community Officials

How we handle occupied properties.

This is the question that matters most to community leaders, and we answer it directly. Sometimes a property we acquire is occupied — by a tenant who doesn't know the owner has sold, or by an owner-occupant in distress.

Our policy, plainly stated:

We do not immediately displace tenants. If a property is occupied at acquisition, we introduce ourselves, explain the plan, and give the tenant a real choice: stay through the rehab (if safe) and become our tenant at a fair rent, or accept a relocation assistance payment and a 90-day notice period to find another place.

We prefer existing tenants who pay and maintain the property. A long-tenured resident who has been in a house for ten years is not a liability — they are exactly the kind of neighbor that makes a community stable.

We comply with all applicable notice and relocation laws and go beyond them where we can afford to. If a county or city has a right-to-return ordinance, we support it.

We will tell you specifically what happened on any property we have acquired in your jurisdiction. We document every tenant interaction. If a community official or grant partner wants a report on tenant outcomes from our portfolio, we provide it.
