What We Stand For

Three commitments, in plain language.

01

Local contractors first.

Every rehab project goes to contractors who live and work in the same county. Not because it's required — because it's right. The money should stay in the community.

02

Attainable rents.

We target rents that a healthcare worker, a school bus driver, or a longtime resident can actually afford. A rehabbed house that only a transplant can afford is not community investment.

03

Existing residents get first consideration.

When a rehabbed property is ready to rent, people already living in the neighborhood hear about it first. That is not a policy — it is the point.

The Problem We're Working On

Watching people get pushed out.

Working on distressed properties in Clayton, we watched families face being pushed out of the towns they had spent their lives in. Not because they wanted to leave. Because the housing stock deteriorated, rents elsewhere climbed, and no one was investing in their blocks.

That is the problem this side of the company was built to work on. We acquire properties that would otherwise sit vacant or deteriorate further, rehabilitate them to a livable standard, and rent them at rates that serve the people who already live in the neighborhood.

This is not charity. It is a business model. A well-maintained rental property in a stable community appreciates. A vacant, blighted property drags everything around it down. We are building a portfolio that proves these goals are not in conflict.
